# Art direction

## Approved idea
Experimental textile graphics × contemporary fashion editorial. The exhibition treats cloth as something to enter, inspect on both faces, rearrange, contextualize, and take away as a print. The experience should feel authored and tactile through composition, material detail, typography, and deliberate interaction—not through effects for their own sake.

## Visual system
- Palette from `Design/DESIGN-TOKENS.json`: paper `#F2EDE5`, cream `#F9F5ED`, ink/plum `#211725`/`#201523`, vermilion `#C54E32`, cobalt `#143D67`, saffron `#E3AC38`, cotton `#E4D4B8`, moss `#586557`, lilac `#BAA7CF`.
- Display: GFS Didot, a high contrast Didone with Greek/Latin character. Use generously, with careful line lengths and mobile sizes; verify body copy legibility. Candidate open fonts: GFS Didot or Prata for display; Arimo or Public Sans for UI/body; Cousine for sparse captions. Confirm actual chosen file and license at implementation.
- Graphic language: visible yarn crossings, irregular blocks, woven rhythm, paper, print edges, restrained rules, large editorial type. Keep the approved colors and materials consistent while each page earns its own composition.
- Motion should expose a meaningful change of face, crossing, repeat, or print—not act as ambient decoration.

## Six experiences
- **Entrance — “Turn it over.”** Split paper-and-textile threshold, oversized title and one clear “Lift the edge” invitation. Desktop is asymmetrical with a broad concept image; mobile becomes an image-led full-viewport cover with overlaid title and bottom action. Animate the edge reveal only after activation; provide a direct button and no-motion state.
- **Loom — “A fabric takes shape.”** Editorial title and legend frame an original loom diagram; controls select weft color and structure, append a row, undo/reset. Desktop gives the loom dominant central space with side controls; mobile stacks title, loom, color swatches, and row actions. Distinguish a visual teaching model from real loom simulation.
- **Reverse — “The back tells another story.”** Large textile inspection area, an explicit front/reverse toggle, and a plain-weave crossing schematic with explanatory copy. Desktop pairs image and explanation; mobile leads with image/toggle then a dark explanatory panel. Current image is concept imagery and cannot prove actual face correspondence.
- **Pattern Room — “Same threads. New arrangements.”** Full-bleed abstract woven graphic and one compact transformation tray. Desktop overlays a broad canvas with repeat/scale/color/structure controls; mobile puts the graphic first and a bottom control panel below. Define controls concretely: repeat, weave rule, weft palette, scale, apply.
- **Collection — “The fabric has a history.”** Museum object record, editorial navigation by objects/techniques/materials/places, and accurate source facts. Desktop places the object image between editorial intro and object label; mobile gives the image and caption priority. Replace illustration with Met image 66.122.20 and clearly separate institutional facts from studio interpretation.
- **Edition — “From thread to print.”** A designed print preview with composition, paper, format, finish options and export. Desktop treats the sheet as an art object with a narrow settings rail; mobile centers the print and makes choices/actions thumb-friendly. Export actual user-selected artwork; clarify output dimensions and format before download.

## Content rules
Use direct, sensory language and accurate object records. Avoid claims of cultural origin, period, maker, material, technique, or provenance unless a cited institutional record supports them. Label studio-made images as studies. Keep page-to-page navigation consistent, but vary each page’s hierarchy and geometry.
